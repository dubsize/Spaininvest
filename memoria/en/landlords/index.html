What we do

From the listing to the keys, then throughout the lease

  1. Bilingual listingCopy in Spanish and English (plus French or Hebrew where useful), the neighbourhood presented for an expat audience, targeted distribution, including to the employers we work with.
  2. Applicant screeningA first conversation with every applicant, fit checked (budget, length of stay, household, pets), grouped viewings.
  3. Application checksEmployment contract or assignment letter, payslips, ID; consistency checks and, where the employer agrees, direct confirmation with HR.
  4. Suitable guaranteesFor applicants without a Spanish history: bank guarantee, additional deposit, resident guarantor, employer commitment — within the limits of the LAU.
  5. Bilingual LAU leaseA contract compliant with the current urban leases act, the Spanish version prevailing with a side-by-side translation; furniture inventory attached.
  6. Fianza depositedThe statutory fianza collected and lodged with the Comunidad de Madrid, as the regulations require.
  7. Inventory and key handoverRoom-by-room inventory with dated photos, meter readings, report signed by both parties.
  8. Point of contact during the leaseThe tenant calls us, not you: small requests, tradespeople, utility transfers, then lease end and check-out.

Guarantees

Securing an applicant with no Spanish track record

A relocated executive has no long Spanish payroll history and no local credit record. Here is what the law allows you to ask for, and what we put in place.

Statutory fianzaOne month's rent for a residential lease (two for other uses, including seasonal lets), lodged with the Comunidad de Madrid.
Additional guaranteeDeposit or bank guarantee. For a five-year residential lease (seven if the landlord is a company), capped at two months' rent.
Resident guarantorSomeone living in Spain stands as joint guarantor for the tenant (fiador).
Employer commitmentA letter from the employer, or even a lease in the company's name: the strongest option for a relocation.
Rent default insuranceOptional, at your expense, issued after the insurer reviews the application.

Worth knowing: the law prohibits requiring more than one month's rent paid in advance. The “six months upfront” sometimes offered is not a guarantee we can recommend.

Landlords' questions

How long does it take to let?

For a fairly priced property in a sought-after area, two to four weeks from listing to signing. We give you a frank view on the rent before we start.

Can I let furnished, or for a few months?

Yes. A seasonal lease (temporada: under a year, for a temporary need of the tenant) follows different rules from a residential lease; we tell you which one fits your situation and the applicant's.

What if the rent is not paid?

We chase immediately and call on the guarantees in the lease. If proceedings are needed, our partner lawyer handles them. Thorough checks are there to avoid getting that far.

I live outside Spain. Is that a problem?

No, it is common: remote signing, inventory without you, photo report, and the tenant has someone on the ground.
